## Framework

The Signal measures every position not in nominal currency terms, but in ounces of gold. This approach strips out the distorting effects of monetary inflation and currency fluctuation, revealing whether a holding is genuinely appreciating in real purchasing power or merely drifting with a depreciating dollar. Gold at $4,020.55 / €3,516.01 per troy ounce serves as this week's benchmark.

Entry and exit discipline is guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14), applied as follows:

| RSI-14 Range | Signal |
|---|---|
| Below 40 | **BUY** — oversold, consider accumulating |
| 40 – 70 | **HOLD** — neutral momentum, no new action |
| Above 70 | **NO ENTRY** — overbought, avoid adding exposure |

Positions are reviewed every Monday. All gold-oz valuations use the EUR gold spot price at time of snapshot.

## This week

Semiconductor and nuclear-adjacent equities are broadly softer heading into the third week of July, with the portfolio's five positions all posting negative weekly changes ranging from roughly 1% to nearly 3%. This broad-based pullback appears to reflect continued recalibration in the technology sector as markets digest the implications of a persistently elevated interest rate environment alongside mixed early signals from the Q2 earnings season. With gold itself trading at elevated levels — now firmly above $4,000 — the oz-gold column is a useful reminder that even flat nominal prices often represent quiet real-terms erosion.

The macro context remains consequential. The EUR/USD rate at 1.1435 reflects a dollar that has ceded meaningful ground over recent months, which flatters the EUR-denominated oz-gold readings for U.S.-listed names such as TSM, NVDA, AMD, and OKLO. Investors holding these positions in euros should be aware that a portion of any nominal gain — or cushion against any nominal loss — is attributable to currency translation rather than underlying business performance. ASML, reporting natively in euros, is insulated from this effect and remains the portfolio's single largest gold-denominated holding at 0.4347 oz.

Looking ahead, earnings reports from major semiconductor customers and continued developments around AI infrastructure spending are the most significant near-term catalysts. TSMC in particular is worth watching closely: the company's revenue trajectory is a bellwether for the entire supply chain, and any guidance commentary on advanced-node demand — particularly from AI accelerator customers — will have read-through implications for NVDA and AMD as well. OKLO's price action remains largely detached from semiconductor cycles and is more sensitive to regulatory news flow from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission and any developments in data-centre power procurement agreements.

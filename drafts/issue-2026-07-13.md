## Framework

The Signal evaluates equity positions not in nominal currency terms, but relative to gold — a discipline that strips out monetary inflation and currency distortion to reveal whether holdings are genuinely appreciating in real purchasing-power terms. Each position is sized and tracked in troy ounces of gold, providing a hard-money baseline for performance assessment.

Entry and exit discipline is guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14), applied as follows:

| RSI-14 Range | Signal |
|---|---|
| Below 40 | **BUY** — oversold; accumulation zone |
| 40 – 70 | **HOLD** — neutral momentum; no new capital deployed |
| Above 70 | **NO ENTRY** — overbought; avoid initiating or adding |

## This week

Gold continues to trade at elevated levels — $4,067.91 USD per troy ounce — reflecting persistent demand for hard assets against a backdrop of ongoing monetary uncertainty and geopolitical friction. With the EUR/USD rate at 1.1430, European-based investors are carrying a meaningful currency headwind on USD-denominated positions, which partly explains the muted or slightly negative euro-adjusted returns on TSM and ASML despite those companies maintaining sound operational fundamentals. The strong dollar relative to recent historical norms warrants continued attention when sizing any new exposure.

Within the semiconductor complex, NVIDIA and AMD are both posting constructive weekly gains — +4.03% and +2.04% respectively — likely buoyed by continued capital flows into AI infrastructure themes and any incremental positive data-points from the enterprise spending cycle. Neither position, however, has yet approached overbought RSI territory, meaning the momentum is orderly rather than speculative. TSMC and ASML remain essentially flat on the week in euro terms, consistent with their more measured, capital-equipment and foundry-oriented business profiles. Both names are consolidating within a neutral RSI band, suggesting institutional positioning rather than active directional conviction.

The notable development this week is OKLO, the advanced nuclear energy developer, which has dipped into BUY territory with an RSI-14 of 38.0. The modest price decline of -0.85% in isolation is less significant than the technical signal it generates when combined with the broader macro context: secular interest in next-generation nuclear power — particularly small modular reactors — remains structurally intact, driven by AI data-centre energy demand and policy support in several major economies. The current RSI reading suggests short-term selling pressure has created a technically oversold condition, which the framework identifies as a disciplined accumulation opportunity.

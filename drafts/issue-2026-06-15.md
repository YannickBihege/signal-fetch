## Framework

The Signal measures every position not in nominal currency terms but in ounces of gold. This approach strips out the distortions of monetary inflation and currency fluctuation, anchoring portfolio performance to a hard, politically neutral unit of account. A position that rises in euros but falls in gold ounces is, by this framework, losing real purchasing power. Only when a holding appreciates against gold is genuine wealth being created.

Entry and exit discipline is guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14), applied as follows:

| RSI-14 | Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Below 40 | **BUY** | Oversold — favourable entry relative to gold |
| 40 – 70 | **HOLD** | Neutral momentum — maintain existing position |
| Above 70 | **NO ENTRY** | Overbought — avoid initiating or adding |

## This week

Semiconductor equities continue to reflect a bifurcated market. AMD stands out this week with a notably sharp single-session gain of +4.73%, likely driven by renewed enthusiasm around its data-centre GPU roadmap and ongoing speculation that hyperscaler customers are diversifying procurement away from a single supplier. At an RSI of 60.5, the move has brought the stock meaningfully higher within its HOLD band but has not yet pushed into overbought territory. ASML, by contrast, slipped 1.89%, a modest pullback that is consistent with the periodic digestion this name has experienced after strong multi-week runs. With an RSI of 65.9, it remains the closest position to the 70-threshold and warrants careful monitoring heading into next week.

On the macro front, gold itself continues to perform as a de facto reserve asset, holding above $4,300 USD per ounce. The EUR/USD rate at 1.1567 reflects a dollar that has ceded meaningful ground over recent months, which mechanically compresses the gold-denominated values of USD-priced holdings for euro-based investors. This currency dynamic partially explains why positions such as NVDA and OKLO appear modest in oz-gold terms despite holding their nominal prices. Investors should remain attentive to the EUR/USD trajectory, as any further dollar weakness would continue to erode the real, gold-benchmarked value of dollar-denominated positions absent corresponding price appreciation.

OKLO remains the smallest position in gold-oz terms at just 0.0132 oz, reflecting both its lower share price and the ongoing premium the market assigns to speculative nuclear micro-reactor development timelines. An RSI of 42.9 keeps it comfortably within HOLD territory, though it sits closer to the BUY threshold than any other name in the portfolio. Should broader risk-off sentiment weigh on the name in coming sessions, a BUY signal could emerge. Macro catalysts to watch this week include any Federal Reserve commentary around the June policy path and European PMI data, both of which have the potential to shift the EUR/USD rate and, by extension, the gold-adjusted value of this entire portfolio.

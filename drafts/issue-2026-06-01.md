## Framework

The Signal measures every position not in nominal currency terms, but in ounces of gold. This approach strips out the noise of monetary inflation and currency fluctuation, anchoring valuations to a hard, supply-constrained asset. A stock that rises in dollar terms but falls in gold terms is, in real purchasing-power terms, losing ground.

Position sizing, entry discipline, and exit timing are all guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14) applied to each ticker's price action. The rules are straightforward:

| RSI-14 Reading | Signal Zone | Action Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Below 40 | BUY | Initiate or add to position |
| 40 – 70 | HOLD | Maintain existing position; no new capital deployed |
| Above 70 | NO ENTRY | Do not initiate or add; reassess existing exposure |

This framework does not require a view on short-term price direction. It requires only that capital is deployed when assets are relatively depressed and preserved when assets are technically extended.

## This week

Four of the five portfolio names posted negative weekly changes, with OKLO leading declines at -1.78% and TSM and NVDA each shedding roughly 1.5%. The moves are not dramatic in isolation, but they are consistent with a broader rotation out of technology growth names as market participants reassess rate expectations heading into the summer. Gold itself continues to serve as a meaningful headwind for equity valuations expressed in ounce terms: with spot holding above $4,500, a stock must appreciate at a meaningful clip simply to tread water in real terms.

ASML stands as the lone bright spot this week, edging up 0.44% to €1,385.06 and retaining its position as the portfolio's largest gold-equivalent holding at 0.3583 oz. The Dutch lithography monopoly continues to benefit from structural demand for advanced EUV capacity, and its relative resilience against a weaker tape is worth noting. TSM, which depends heavily on ASML tooling for its leading-edge nodes, mirrors ASML's RSI reading of 59.5, suggesting both names are travelling through similar technical territory without either being stretched to the upside.

The macro backdrop for the week is shaped by several converging factors: the first trading day of June historically brings portfolio rebalancing flows, while the semiconductor sector broadly awaits further clarity on US export licensing policy toward China — a risk that weighs most directly on NVDA and TSM. OKLO, the sole nuclear-energy name in the portfolio, trades on its own catalysts, with investors monitoring any regulatory developments from the Nuclear Regulatory Commission. Its RSI of 50.7 reflects a market that is genuinely undecided on near-term direction.

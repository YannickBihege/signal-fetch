## Framework

The Signal measures every position not in dollars or euros, but in troy ounces of gold. This approach strips out the noise of currency fluctuation and monetary expansion, expressing each holding's true purchasing-power weight. A stock must appreciate faster than gold to justify its place in the portfolio; if it cannot, capital is better preserved in the metal itself.

Entry and exit discipline is guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14) applied to each position's USD price. The rules are simple and applied without discretion:

| RSI-14 Range | Signal |
|---|---|
| RSI < 40 | **BUY** — oversold, accumulate |
| RSI 40–70 | **HOLD** — trend intact, no new entry |
| RSI > 70 | **NO ENTRY** — overbought, wait for reset |

All gold valuations use the current spot price of **€3,800.70 EUR per troy ounce** (gold spot: $4,355.60 USD; EUR/USD: 1.1460).

## This week

Markets enter the week of September 21st with semiconductor and nuclear energy names broadly advancing, set against a backdrop of continued monetary policy uncertainty. Gold itself remains firmly bid at $4,355.60, a level that keeps the bar for equity outperformance meaningfully high. The EUR/USD rate at 1.1460 reflects a relatively strong euro, which compresses the gold-equivalent valuations of US-listed holdings when measured in European purchasing power terms — a headwind worth monitoring for EUR-based investors as positions are sized.

The semiconductor complex is the standout theme this week. ASML leads the portfolio with a gain of just over 3%, while AMD follows closely at +2.70%. Both moves are consistent with renewed optimism around equipment order cadence and AI-accelerated chip demand. TSMC and NVIDIA also participated constructively, adding approximately 1.0% and 1.3% respectively. These gains are encouraging in nominal terms, though at gold's current altitude, a 1–3% weekly move in an equity position represents only a modest narrowing of the performance gap against the metal. Investors should resist reading short-term nominal gains as confirmation of sustained real-value creation until RSI levels or gold-relative ratios show a more decisive trend.

The sole detractor this week is OKLO, which shed 4.16% and now sits at just 0.0087 oz of gold — the smallest position in the portfolio by that measure. The pullback is notable but not yet alarming; the RSI of 44.6 keeps the name comfortably within HOLD territory, and the nuclear energy narrative underpinning the thesis remains structurally intact. Nonetheless, the position warrants close observation. A continuation of selling pressure toward an RSI below 40 would shift the signal to BUY, potentially offering a more attractive accumulation point for those with conviction in the long-term power-demand story.

## Framework

The Signal measures every holding not in nominal currency terms, but in ounces of gold. This approach strips out the distortions of monetary inflation and currency fluctuation, expressing each position's true purchasing-power weight. A stock that rises in euros but falls in gold terms is, by this measure, losing ground.

Entry and exit discipline is guided by the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14):

| RSI-14 Range | Signal |
|---|---|
| Below 40 | **BUY** — oversold, consider initiating or adding |
| 40 – 70 | **HOLD** — neutral momentum, maintain position |
| Above 70 | **NO ENTRY** — overbought, avoid adding exposure |

## This week

The first trading week of July has delivered a broad-based pullback across the semiconductor and nuclear energy names in this portfolio. Every position closed lower in EUR terms, with AMD and ASML bearing the steepest corrections, down 4.26% and 4.00% respectively. These moves reflect a wider rotation out of high-multiple technology names as markets continue to recalibrate expectations around interest rate trajectories in both the United States and the eurozone. Gold, meanwhile, holds firm at $4,156.59 — reinforcing its role as the denominator of record. In oz-gold terms, the portfolio's heaviest holding, ASML at 0.4257 oz, has become modestly cheaper in real terms, a development worth monitoring.

Macro conditions remain the dominant driver of short-term noise. The EUR/USD rate of 1.1448 reflects a relatively strong euro, which compresses the EUR-denominated values of these USD-listed equities and partially explains the magnitude of the weekly declines for European investors. On the data front, attention will turn later this week to U.S. labor market figures and any Federal Reserve commentary, both of which have consistently moved semiconductor equities in recent months. ASML, straddling the Atlantic as a Dutch company with heavy USD revenue exposure, faces a dual headwind from both currency dynamics and any softening in forward guidance from its key customers.

OKLO deserves a specific note despite its modest nominal decline of just 0.17%. At 0.0126 oz gold, it remains the smallest position in gold-weight terms, and its RSI of 40.6 is the closest in the portfolio to the BUY threshold of 40.

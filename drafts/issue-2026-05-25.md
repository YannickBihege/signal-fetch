## Framework

The Signal evaluates equity positions not in nominal currency terms, but relative to gold — the benchmark currency of last resort. Each position is sized and tracked in troy ounces of gold, stripping away the distortions of fiat debasement and monetary inflation. A stock that rises in euros but falls in gold ounces is, by this framework, losing real value.

Momentum is assessed using the 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI-14) to avoid adding exposure at overextended levels:

| RSI-14 Range | Signal |
|---|---|
| Below 40 | **BUY** — oversold, favourable entry |
| 40 – 70 | **HOLD** — momentum neutral, maintain positions |
| Above 70 | **NO ENTRY** — overbought, do not add |

## This week

Gold continues to assert itself as the dominant pricing unit, with spot now firmly above $4,500 USD — a level that would have seemed extraordinary not long ago. At €3,939.44 per troy ounce, the metal is compressing the gold-denominated value of equities even as some names post nominal gains. Investors should note that a stock must rise meaningfully in euro terms simply to tread water against a gold benchmark at these levels. The EUR/USD rate of 1.1595 reflects continued dollar softness, which flatters euro-denominated readings but does not alter the gold-ounce arithmetic.

The semiconductor complex is the focal point of this week's price action. AMD's near-4% nominal advance has pushed its RSI into overbought territory, likely driven by a combination of AI infrastructure enthusiasm and short covering. TSM and NVDA, by contrast, have both softened — NVDA in particular declining nearly 2% — suggesting some rotation within the chip space rather than broad sector strength. ASML's 2.6% gain is the more constructive signal: as the sole supplier of EUV lithography equipment, its move higher is underpinned by tangible order visibility rather than sentiment alone.

OKLO, the small-modular reactor development company, continues its quiet grind higher with a modest 1.2% gain and a neutral RSI of 50.1. The position remains the portfolio's smallest in gold-ounce terms (0.0144 oz) and the most speculative. The broader context for nuclear power — rising data-centre electricity demand and policy support for advanced fission — remains supportive of the long-term thesis, though near-term catalysts are limited. No change to positioning is warranted across the majority of the book this week.
